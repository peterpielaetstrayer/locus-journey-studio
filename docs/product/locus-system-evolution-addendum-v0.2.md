# LOCUS System Evolution Addendum v0.2

**Status:** Active product-direction addendum  
**Date:** 2026-07-30  
**Applies to:** OWLL / LOCUS Learning Architecture Charter v0.1, LOCUS Journey Studio, future LOCUS Core applications

---

## 0. Purpose

This addendum records how the LOCUS vision and product architecture have evolved through the Virginia Beach prototype, the Living Curriculum use case, progressive learner modeling, universal input, free exploration, cohort personalization, and spatial authoring.

It does not discard the OWLL / LOCUS Learning Architecture Charter v0.1. The charter remains the learning-science and design doctrine. This document supersedes or clarifies implementation assumptions that have become too narrow.

The central evolution is:

> LOCUS is not only a place-based journey product, Field Notes tool, AI tutor, curriculum generator, or LMS. LOCUS is a learner-owned adaptive educational interface between a person and the world.

It should help any question, place, source, project, memory, problem, conversation, object, or intention become an opening into meaningful action, structured learning, reflection, creation, memory, and growth.

---

## 1. Full product horizon

As the world becomes increasingly computable through phones, cameras, wearables, glasses, spatial interfaces, and future devices, LOCUS should become its educational layer.

The long-horizon promise is:

> Wherever a person is, LOCUS can help them notice more, understand more deeply, act more capably, create evidence, remember what matters, and continue becoming.

This does not mean covering reality with constant labels or explanations. LOCUS should heighten perception without obstructing the world.

---

## 2. The primary interaction

The primary interface is not a conventional dashboard or chatbot. It is a universal multimodal input that allows a learner to bring almost anything into LOCUS.

The canonical interaction spine is:

```text
Touch
→ Express
→ Interpret
→ Act
→ Structure
→ Remember
→ Adapt
```

### Touch

LOCUS becomes available through one deliberate action.

### Express

The learner may speak, type, show, photograph, upload, share, point, or ask.

### Interpret

LOCUS considers the immediate input, current context, learner model, active journeys, projects, place, time, and available device capabilities.

### Act

LOCUS makes meaningful activity easier now. It may ask one useful question, begin a learning session, suggest an observation, produce a next action, create a field challenge, or help clarify a decision.

### Structure

The interaction may become a Field Note, Pathway, Journey, activity, reflection, artifact, decision, connection, or spatial learning object.

### Remember

LOCUS preserves what matters, records provenance, and determines whether the idea should resurface.

### Adapt

The learner model, pathway state, and future support become more accurate over time.

---

## 3. Stable bones and adaptive skin

LOCUS should be highly personalizable without becoming unpredictable.

> LOCUS has stable bones and an adaptive skin.

### Stable bones

Likely durable system areas include:

- universal input
- Home
- Pathways / Journeys
- Field Notes / Capture Inbox
- Atlas
- Learning Profile
- privacy, memory, and presence controls

### Adaptive skin

LOCUS may personalize:

- aesthetic theme
- information density
- primary modality
- navigation emphasis
- journey presentation
- prompt frequency
- explanation depth
- degree of structure
- maps, timelines, notebooks, constellations, studios, or spatial views

The AI may propose interface adaptations, but the learner should remain able to inspect, approve, reject, or reverse them.

---

## 4. Progressive learner discovery

LOCUS should not require a learner to complete a large profile before receiving value.

> Help now. Learn the learner over time.

A learner may deliberately complete a fuller profile, but they may also begin through free exploration, a single intention, a joined journey, a Field Note, or an immediate problem.

The learner model may develop from:

- directly stated information
- imported learning history
- demonstrated knowledge and skill
- artifacts and evidence
- recurring choices and behavior
- learner-approved inferences
- mentor or teacher input
- spatial and field experiences
- unresolved questions and active projects

Every learner-model claim should preserve:

- source
- confidence
- recency
- visibility
- whether it is stated, imported, demonstrated, or inferred

The learner must be able to correct, delete, privatize, or challenge what LOCUS believes.

---

## 5. Pathways and Journeys

**Pathway** is the general LOCUS structure for sustained development.

A pathway may begin from:

- a learning intention
- a skill
- a project
- a personal question
- a developmental goal
- an authored program
- a field experience
- a professional objective

Examples include:

- Psychology for Human Development
- Philosophy Reconstruction
- LOCUS Product Development
- Baseline
- Puerto Rico Cultural Journey

**Journey** is a pathway type with more explicit experience architecture. It may be place-based, time-bounded, cohort-based, creator-authored, or compiled from a wider journey graph.

Examples include:

- Water Writes the Landscape
- a Civil War field journey
- a seven-day or eight-Saturday historical expedition
- a San Diego coastal systems journey

Journeys should be capable of multiple renderings without duplicating the underlying learning architecture.

---

## 6. Field Notes evolution

Field Notes remains a focused, independently useful product loop, but should be treated architecturally as a universal LOCUS capture primitive.

A Field Note should not always require a journey, cohort, enrollment, or predefined stop.

A capture may begin unclassified and later connect to:

- one or more pathways
- a journey
- a concept
- a place
- a project
- an artifact
- a person
- a future review

Field Notes should support both spontaneous capture and structured evidence inside an authored journey.

## 7. Product topology

The current recommended topology is:

### LOCUS Core

The main learner-owned surface for:

- universal input
- personal pathways
- progressive learner modeling
- project memory and decision support
- psychology and philosophy reconstruction
- free-form Field Notes
- Atlas and resurfacing
- adaptive interface
- future personal spatial interaction

### LOCUS Journey Studio

The specialized creator, learner, orchestrator, and reviewer environment for:

- authored place-based journeys
- route and stop architecture
- cohorts
- adaptive branches
- evidence and artifacts
- mentor intervention
- field testing and review
- Virginia Beach and future creator journeys
- eventual spatial authoring

### Shared contracts

LOCUS Core and Journey Studio should share definitions and import/export contracts for:

- learner
- pathway
- journey
- activity
- capture
- evidence
- artifact
- concept
- connection
- adaptation
- resurfacing
- spatial anchor
- learner-model claim

Do not force the two applications into one codebase before these contracts are stable.

## 8. Spatial learning modes

LOCUS should support three major spatial modes.

### Guided Journey

A creator defines the spine, essential anchors, questions, actions, evidence, safety, accessibility, and intended arc. LOCUS personalizes within creator-defined boundaries.

### Free Exploration

The learner enters a place without a predetermined route. LOCUS uses learner context, visible environment, location, prior learning, and presence settings to surface occasional high-value opportunities.

Free Exploration should feel like heightened perception, not constant narration.

### Live Authoring

A creator stands in the environment and collaboratively authors the learning layer with LOCUS.

The creator may:

- point or ask, “Do you see this?”
- capture a place or phenomenon
- speak a learning possibility
- define what should be noticed first
- attach evidence requirements
- identify safety or accessibility issues
- create an artifact prompt
- test the learner experience immediately
- publish or keep the result private

The long-term interaction is:

```text
See
→ Point
→ Speak
→ Interpret
→ Shape
→ Test
→ Publish
```

## 9. Portable spatial learning artifacts

LOCUS should eventually create AR and spatial learning artifacts in real time.

A spatial learning artifact is not merely a 3D object or coordinate. It should contain:

### Semantic core

- learning intention
- concept or phenomenon
- what the learner should notice
- learner action
- evidence expectation
- reflection or artifact opportunity

### Creator intent

- fixed elements
- adaptable elements
- audience
- non-negotiable claims
- review requirements

### Spatial binding

- geographic or environmental reference
- orientation and scale
- trigger conditions
- visual anchor
- safety and accessibility notes

### Adaptation policy

- which elements may change
- acceptable environmental equivalents
- personalization boundaries
- cohort rules
- creator approval requirements

### Rendering options

- text card
- audio prompt
- map point
- camera overlay
- phone AR
- glasses interface
- future spatial display

### Provenance

- creator
- sources
- place of origin
- version
- adaptation history
- maturity status
- review status

## 10. Cross-place adaptation

LOCUS may identify that a learning interaction created in one place can be adapted to another.

Example:

A Virginia Beach activity asks learners to infer the movement of water from environmental evidence without directly observing moving water.

In Tahoe, LOCUS may identify a related opportunity involving snowmelt, erosion, drainage, soil, vegetation, and watershed movement.

LOCUS should preserve the semantic learning core while proposing a new spatial binding.

Cross-place adaptation should initially create a private draft, not an automatically published journey component.

The creator should be able to define an adaptation budget:

- framing may adapt
- difficulty may adapt
- capture mode may adapt
- examples may adapt
- location may adapt with review
- required claims may not adapt
- shared cohort anchors may not disappear

---

## 11. Maturity and provenance ladder

AI suggestions, learner observations, creator drafts, and reviewed curriculum must not appear to have equal authority.

Every learning artifact should have a visible maturity status.

```text
1. Ephemeral suggestion
   AI identifies a possible learning opportunity.

2. Personal capture
   A learner or creator saves it privately.

3. Draft artifact
   It has educational structure but has not been tested.

4. Field-tested artifact
   It has been used in the actual environment.

5. Reviewed adaptation
   Factual, educational, safety, and accessibility review occurred.

6. Published artifact
   It is available to a defined audience.

7. Canonical journey component
   It is maintained as part of an authored experience.
```

Status, creator, sources, version, and adaptation history should remain visible.

---

## 12. Shared anchors and personalized edges

Cohort personalization should not isolate learners from one another.

> Shared anchors preserve the cohort. Personalized edges deepen the learner.

A creator may define:

- essential shared encounters
- common questions
- collaborative artifacts
- group discussion points
- fixed safety and logistical boundaries

LOCUS may personalize:

- examples
- modalities
- scaffolding
- challenge level
- optional discoveries
- concept connections
- identity pathways
- retrieval opportunities

Learners should have opportunities to compare what each person noticed or understood differently.

---

## 13. Presence modes

LOCUS may eventually be available across multiple devices and contexts, but ambient presence must remain deliberate and inspectable.

Possible modes:

- **Off:** no observation or collection
- **Available:** one touch or invocation away
- **Capture:** rapid saving with minimal interruption
- **Guide:** active support for the present situation
- **Focus:** one intention, minimal branching
- **Reflect:** consolidation, meaning-making, artifact creation, and resurfacing
- **Creator:** field or studio authoring

The user should always know which mode is active and what data is being used.

LOCUS should begin with current devices while preserving the full spatial horizon.

### Current foundation

- phone and responsive web
- voice, text, images, documents, and links
- GPS and maps
- camera-assisted interpretation
- personal pathways
- structured memory
- field capture
- creator pins and previews

### Near-term spatial work

- phone-based spatial preview
- geospatial anchors
- creator field mode
- shared spatial artifacts
- learner-specific rendering

### Long-horizon interfaces

- hands-free glasses
- contextual visual understanding

## 15. Near-term prototype priorities

The next LOCUS Core alpha should prove:

1. A new user can press one button and speak naturally.
2. LOCUS can interpret whether the input is a question, capture, pathway, project decision, or immediate action need.
3. LOCUS can provide useful activity within seconds.
4. The interaction can become a persistent structured object.
5. Psychology, philosophy, and LOCUS product development can function as real personal pathways.
6. The learner can inspect and correct what LOCUS remembers.
7. Important ideas can resurface later.
8. Journey Studio can eventually export an authored Journey Manifest into LOCUS Core.

The first system should not attempt to prove the entire AR future.

## 16. Current execution horizon

### By September 2026 — target private alpha

A credible target is:

- LOCUS Core Home
- universal text and push-to-talk input
- psychology, philosophy, and LOCUS project pathways
- Capture Inbox / universal Field Notes
- basic learner profile and approved memory updates
- next-action generation
- simple retrieval and resurfacing
- early San Diego free-exploration workflow
- phone-based creator field drafts using image, voice, and location

### By December 2026 — target private creator alpha

A credible stretch target is:

- stronger personal learner model
- project and decision memory
- grounded source retrieval
- multiple pathway types
- Creator Field Mode
- map-based journey authoring
- Journey Manifest import/export
- controlled local adaptation drafts
- limited phone AR or spatial previews
- invitation access for a small number of trusted educators or creators

The goal is not the completed LOCUS vision by December. The goal is a coherent system that visibly contains its genetic code.

## 18. Superseded or clarified assumptions

This addendum clarifies the following earlier assumptions:

1. Field Notes may remain a focused product loop, but should not become an architecturally disconnected system.
2. The general LOCUS learner shell should not be forced into the current Virginia Beach interface.
3. Journey Studio should continue as a specialized vertical slice while LOCUS Core is developed.
4. Live AI and connected persistence may now be used where they support a bounded private alpha.
5. AR is not required for the first proof, but spatial artifact and adaptation contracts should be designed now.
6. A dashboard is not the primary interaction; universal input is primary, while Home supports orientation and continuity.
7. Personalization includes interface, modality, cognitive support, developmental goals, and contextual role—not merely recommendations.

---

## 19. Closing anchor

LOCUS should make meaningful learning activity easier to begin, easier to continue, and harder to lose.

Its near-term proof is simple:

> A person touches one button, expresses what is happening, receives meaningful support toward action, preserves what matters, and becomes better understood over time.

Its long-term horizon is larger:

> A person can move through any place, project, question, or situation and encounter an adaptive educational layer that helps the world become more legible, actionable, memorable, and meaningful.

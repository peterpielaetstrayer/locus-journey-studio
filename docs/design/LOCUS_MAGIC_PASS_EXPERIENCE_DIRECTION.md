# LOCUS Magic Pass
## Experience Direction and Six-Screen North-Star Storyboard

**Version:** 0.1  
**Purpose:** Transform the current LOCUS prototype from a competent software demonstration into an emotionally resonant, place-first learning experience without rebuilding its routing, backend, state, or learning architecture.

---

# 1. The problem this pass solves

The current application successfully proves:

- role separation;
- learner progression;
- adaptive logic;
- artifact creation;
- resurfacing;
- creator and orchestrator architecture;
- backend readiness.

But it still feels like a product explaining itself.

The Magic Pass changes the governing experience from:

> “Here are the parts of LOCUS.”

into:

> “You have entered a place that is beginning to reveal itself.”

The next pass is not a feature sprint. It is an **interaction-art-direction sprint**.

---

# 2. North-star feeling

The learner should feel that:

- the real world is deeper than it first appears;
- a quiet intelligence is helping them notice;
- their observations materially change what becomes visible;
- they are discovering a living system rather than completing screens;
- their own thinking is becoming part of a persistent atlas;
- the technology gradually recedes as their perception strengthens.

The creator should feel that:

- they are authoring into the landscape;
- educational judgment remains visible and respected;
- AI proposes structure but does not determine the journey;
- a place can be shaped into a rigorous learning experience without reducing it to a lesson form.

The orchestrator should feel that:

- they are perceiving learners, not monitoring metrics;
- the system helps them see where attention, evidence, confidence, and misunderstanding are diverging;
- intervention is a thoughtful human act, not an alert-handling task.

---

# 3. Core design doctrine

## Reality comes first

A learner enters a landscape, not a dashboard.

## Mystery comes before explanation

Every journey begins with something unresolved.

## The landscape is the interface

Maps, prompts, notes, and system diagrams should feel attached to the world rather than layered on top of a generic app shell.

## The interface responds to evidence

Meaningful observations should reveal routes, clues, relationships, and artifact elements.

## AI speaks only when it adds something

No automatic paragraphs. No constant tutoring. No conversational filler.

## Quiet is an interaction

Silence, waiting, noticing, and visual restraint are intentional product states.

## The learner’s thinking is the collectible

The reward is not points. It is a growing personal model of the world.

## The product should eventually make itself less necessary

LOCUS succeeds when learners begin noticing more without prompts.

# 4. Emotional arc of Water Writes the Landscape

| Phase | Emotional movement | Product behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival | Ordinary place → charged place | Minimal cinematic entrance |
| Invitation | Curiosity → mission | One unresolved question |
| First evidence | Uncertainty → agency | Capture changes the map |
| Mystery | Confidence → productive doubt | LOCUS asks for stronger evidence |
| Quiet attention | Searching → seeing | Interface fades almost entirely |
| Systems revelation | Fragments → coherence | Learner evidence assembles into causal structure |
| Artifact | Completion → ownership | Work becomes a durable atlas page |
| Resurfacing | Memory → transfer | Idea returns in a new environment |

# 5. Six north-star screens

These six screens define the product’s soul. They should be designed and implemented before attempting to beautify every route.

---

## Screen 1 — Enter the Landscape

### Purpose

Replace the explanatory software gateway with a place-first emotional entrance.

### Visual composition

- Full viewport image or video loop of a First Landing wetland at dawn.
- Water, cypress trunks, mist, reflections, boardwalk edge.
- Darkened lower third for legibility.
- No visible dashboard cards.
- LOCUS mark is small and quiet.
- A restrained “Studio” access control remains available for Creator, Orchestrator, and Reviewer modes.

### Primary copy

```text
FIRST LANDING
7:18 AM

The water looks still.

It isn’t.
```

### Primary action

> Enter the landscape

### Secondary action

> Open Journey Studio

### Motion

- Very subtle water movement or slow parallax.
- Copy enters in three beats.
- No loading spinner unless genuinely necessary.
- Reduced-motion version uses a still image and opacity transitions.

### Sound

Optional, off by default:

- faint water;
- distant birds;
- wind in leaves.

### Emotional target

> “Something is here that I have not learned to see yet.”

### Do not

- explain OWLL, LOCUS, and Field Notes here;
- show four role cards;
- display feature lists;
- use a loud onboarding tour.

---

## Screen 2 — The Journey Awakens

### Purpose

Turn the central question into a mission and reveal only the beginning of the route.

### Visual composition

- Wetland scene remains dominant.
- A thin, almost hand-drawn route line emerges from the foreground.
- Only the first two anchors are visible.
- The rest of the route dissolves into atmospheric fog.
- A small Field Guide card indicates what will be created.

### Primary copy

```text
WATER WRITES THE LANDSCAPE

Find evidence of water
without looking at the water.
```

### Supporting copy

> Collect evidence. Test an explanation. Leave with one page of a living field guide.

### Actions

- Begin walking
- Prepare first
- Accessibility and route options

### Interaction

Before the route unlocks, the learner records a baseline thought:

> Water shapes a place by…

The baseline is stored but not judged.

### Emotional target

> “I have a real mission, and the place is withholding something from me.”

---

## Screen 3 — Water Fingerprint Capture

### Purpose

Make the first successful observation feel consequential.

### Visual composition

- Camera/image capture dominates.
- Prompt is small and anchored near the bottom.
- The learner can photograph, speak, write, or sketch.
- The image becomes a tactile Field Note card rather than a generic form submission.

### Primary prompt

> Find evidence of water that is not water itself.

### Capture sequence

1. Learner captures dark soil, exposed roots, plant distribution, or trail elevation.
2. LOCUS asks:

   > What did you notice?

3. Learner responds.
4. LOCUS asks one evidence question only:

   > What nearby comparison could strengthen or challenge that idea?

### Success response

```text
A fingerprint.

You found water
without photographing water.
```

### World response

- One region of the map clears.
- The Field Note pins itself to the route.
- A causal-thread seed appears: `water → soil`.

### Emotional target

> “My observation changed the world I can see.”

### Do not

- use confetti;
- award arbitrary points;
- say “Correct!”;
- show a long AI explanation.

---

## Screen 4 — Cypress-Knee Mystery

### Purpose

Create the defining hypothesis-and-uncertainty moment.

### Visual composition

- Full-bleed close environmental image.
- Cypress knees are unmistakably present.
- The prompt appears as a small field label, not a quiz panel.
- Learner theories appear as movable note fragments or a voice waveform.

### Primary copy

```text
These structures are doing something.

What do you think they are doing?
```

### Learner response modes

- Speak a theory
- Sketch it
- Type it
- Choose “I’m not sure yet”

### LOCUS response logic

If the learner gives a confident theory with weak evidence:

> What would you need to observe before trusting that explanation?

If the learner is uncertain but observant:

> Start with one possibility. What detail made you consider it?

If the learner needs movement:

> Find the strangest example within ten safe steps.

If advanced:

> Design a field comparison that could separate two competing explanations.

### Visual consequence

The learner’s theory becomes a translucent layer over the image. New evidence can sharpen, distort, or revise it.

### Emotional target

> “I am allowed to form a theory, but I am responsible for testing it.”

---

## Screen 5 — The Hidden Flow

### Purpose

Demonstrate that stillness and attention are first-class interactions.

### Visual composition

- Nearly empty interface.
- A calm wetland overlook.
- No progress bar.
- No visible navigation for the first phase except an unobtrusive exit/accessibility control.

### Primary copy

```text
For two minutes,
stop trying to move forward.
```

### Learner chooses

- Listen
- Watch one small area
- Sit in silence

### During the interval

The interface fades to almost nothing.

Optional indicators:

- a subtle breathing light;
- a thin environmental waveform;
- no ticking countdown unless accessibility requires it.

### Return prompt

> What became visible when you stopped searching?

### Possible captured observations

- insect movement;
- reflection changes;
- sound layers;
- bubbles;
- drifting material;
- light shifts;
- subtle movement of leaves or surface water.

### Emotional target

> “Attention itself changed what the place contained.”

---

## Screen 6 — The Living Atlas Artifact

### Purpose

Turn accumulated evidence into ownership, memory, and future identity.

### Visual composition

- A full-page natural-history atlas spread.
- Parchment, ink, image fragments, causal lines, map coordinates, date, time.
- Learner evidence appears visibly authored by them.
- The systems map emerges from the route and captured observations.

### Artifact contents

- Learner-created title
- Selected photograph or sketch
- Original hypothesis
- Three strongest evidence fragments
- Revised explanation
- Systems diagram
- Remaining uncertainty
- One connection to another place
- Exact location and time
- Emerging identity marker

### Systems reveal

```text
rain
  ↓
water level
  ↓
soil saturation
  ↓
plant distribution
  ↓
habitat
  ↓
where humans can walk
```

The learner should recognize that the boardwalk and route were also part of the system.

### Completion copy

```text
You did not finish a lesson.

You learned to read one part of the world.
```

### Next action

> Add this page to my Virginia Beach Atlas

### Emotional target

> “This is evidence that I can see and explain more than I could before.”

# 6. Persistent visual language

## Environmental layers

Every major learner screen should combine three layers:

1. **Place** — image, video, environmental sound, map texture
2. **Inquiry** — one question or action
3. **Evidence** — learner-authored capture that persists

The product should never lead with interface chrome.

## Route language

The map should feel like a living topographic sketch:

- hand-drawn route line;
- atmospheric fog;
- field-note pins;
- small causal threads;
- no conventional blue navigation line;
- no emphasis on shortest path or speed.

## Field Notes

Field Notes should feel collected, not submitted.

Use:

- pinned photographs;
- taped edges;
- small coordinates;
- timestamps;
- handwritten-style annotation only as an accent;
- confidence shown as language, not a score.

## Typography

- Sans serif for navigation and interface.
- Serif for field-guide moments, quotations, and artifacts.
- Large quiet type over imagery.
- Avoid overusing all caps.

## Motion

Motion should reveal relationships rather than decorate.

Approved:

- fog clearing;
- route drawing;
- evidence pinning;
- systems lines assembling;
- card unfolding;
- subtle parallax;
- quiet fades.

Avoid:

- bouncing buttons;
- constant animated backgrounds;
- confetti;
- exaggerated page transitions;
- attention-seeking loaders.

# 7. Navigation redesign

## Public learner entrance

The public root route should become the cinematic entrance.

## Studio entrance

A restrained control opens:

- Creator Studio
- Orchestrator View
- Reviewer View
- Demo reset

This can be a quiet top-right control labeled:

> Studio

## Learner navigation

During the field journey:

- no persistent desktop sidebar;
- mobile-safe bottom controls only when necessary;
- map, capture, guide, and help;
- current place and safety access remain available;
- the interface may disappear during quiet-attention moments.

---

# 8. Creator Mode north star

Creator Mode should be divided into two environments.

## Field Authoring

Designed for phone/tablet in place.

Flow:

```text
Capture the place
→ speak the learning possibility
→ LOCUS proposes structure
→ creator accepts, rejects, or reshapes
→ save as field draft
```

The creator can capture:

- exact place;
- image;
- voice thought;
- learner action;
- likely evidence;
- safety observation;
- accessibility consideration;
- unexpected field condition.

## Studio Refinement

Designed for desktop/tablet.

Flow:

```text
Place anchor
→ learning intention
→ central question
→ learner action
→ evidence requirement
→ adaptive branches
→ mentor judgment
→ safety/access
→ preview
```

The map and the learner preview should remain visible while editing.

### AI behavior

AI may propose:

- a central question;
- a comparison activity;
- likely misconceptions;
- evidence requirements;
- advanced and reluctant-learner pathways;
- resurfacing connections.

The creator must explicitly approve or edit each proposal.

---

# 9. Orchestrator Mode north star

Orchestrator Mode should become a live evidence table rather than a completion dashboard.

Show:

- where each learner is in the route;
- their latest observation;
- their current theory;
- confidence language;
- evidence strength;
- whether their thinking has changed;
- the system’s reason for recommending an intervention.

Avoid:

- completion percentages as the main signal;
- ranking learners;
- red-alert overload;
- generic analytics charts.

Preferred intervention language:

> Maya has a strong theory and high confidence, but she has not yet looked for contradictory evidence.

Not:

> Maya — 63% complete — intervention recommended.

# 10. Implementation scope for the first Magic Pass

Transform only these routes/components first:

```text
/
/learner
/learner/water-fingerprints
/learner/cypress-knee
/learner/hidden-flow
/learner/systems
/learner/artifact
```

Preserve the existing data and behavior of all other routes.

Add:

- cinematic public entrance;
- journey-awakening invitation;
- environmental media architecture;
- evidence-driven fog clearing;
- tactile Field Note treatment;
- quiet-attention state;
- systems reveal animation;
- atlas artifact redesign;
- Studio access control.

Do not yet redesign the entire Creator and Orchestrator products. Establish the visual and interaction language through the learner pathway first.

---

# 11. Acceptance criteria

The Magic Pass succeeds when:

- the first screen feels like entering a place, not an application;
- a viewer can understand the mission without reading product explanations;
- the Water Fingerprint capture produces a visible world response;
- Cypress-Knee Mystery feels like inquiry rather than a quiz;
- Hidden Flow contains a meaningful low-interface state;
- the final artifact feels valuable enough to keep or print;
- animations reinforce learning relationships;
- reduced-motion mode remains coherent;
- accessibility and safety access remain available;
- all existing tests, state, routes, and backend architecture continue to work;
- the experience remains honest about simulated AI and prototype status.

---

# 12. Final product principle

> LOCUS should not make the world feel augmented by software.
>
> It should make the world feel newly legible.
